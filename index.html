Today the big models rank themselves and ask you to trust them. We build the opposite — an independent, calibrated ranking computed from real evaluations, on a method that's public by design.

How to read it. Two lenses on the same data. Performance ranks purely by calibration (who's most accurate, money no object). Value weights accuracy, speed, and cost — the best trustworthy answer per dollar. Often they disagree: the most accurate model and the best-value model are rarely the same one.

What "Brier" means. Each model answers with a confidence; we score it against known ground truth with a proper scoring rule, so being confidently wrong costs far more than admitting uncertainty. Lower Brier = better-calibrated.

Honest scope. This currently measures code-review discrimination — one narrow proxy — not general trustworthiness. Early data, small samples, rough cost estimates. It's a hypothesis we want attacked: tear it apart in the Trust Commons.
